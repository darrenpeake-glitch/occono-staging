Quick summary

For most UK small businesses that need more than a two-step Zap, start with Make. Stay on Zapier when the Zaps already work, or when two steps and 100 tasks a month are the whole job.

Best for most UK small businesses connecting several apps: Make. Make’s comparison page lists a Free plan at $0 with 1,000 credits a month, and says that plan is not time-limited. The same page lists Core at $9 a month, starting at 10,000 credits. Make’s pricing FAQ says the Free plan is 1,000 credits a month. We earn from Make. The head-to-head with Zapier is the Make and Zapier comparison.

Stay on Zapier when the workflows are already built, or when one trigger and one action is enough. Zapier’s pricing page lists Free at $0 with 100 tasks a month, and Professional at $19.99 a month billed annually or $29.99 billed monthly (750 tasks). The same page says there is a 14-day free trial of Professional, with no credit card required. We do not earn from Zapier.

Best when the business already pays for Microsoft 365 and the flow stays on standard connectors: Power Automate. Microsoft’s licensing FAQ lists Office 365 Business Basic, Business Standard and Business Premium among the licences that include Power Automate, for standard connectors. Premium is £11.50 per user a month, paid yearly, and the UK page says the price does not include VAT. We do not earn from Microsoft. The connector detail is the Make and Power Automate comparison.

n8n, Pabbly Connect and Activepieces suit a narrower reader: someone who will run the software on their own server, someone chasing a low dollar price for a large number of action steps, or someone who wants one credit per whole run. IFTTT suits two simple connections. None of those four is the default for a typical UK small business. The wider list of tools is the AI automation tools guide.

A worked example, Occono’s own arithmetic, from the rules above. A website enquiry is added to a sheet and an email is sent. That is a trigger and two actions. On Zapier’s free plan the workflow is limited to one action, so this job needs Professional. If each successful action is a task, 100 enquiries are about 200 tasks, inside the 750 on the $19.99 annual Professional tier. On Make, if each of the three modules uses 1 credit, 100 enquiries are about 300 credits, inside the Free plan’s 1,000. Make’s FAQ also counts a scheduled check that reads data, so a quiet week can still use credits. On n8n, 100 enquiries are 100 executions, inside the Starter tier’s 2,500, and also inside the cloud trial’s 1,000. On Activepieces, if a run is one credit, 100 enquiries are 100 credits. On Pabbly, if the trigger is free and each action is one task, 100 enquiries are about 200 tasks, which is over the free 100 and inside the Standard 10,000. The arithmetic changes as soon as a workflow adds a branch, a lookup or an AI step. Count the real steps on one live week before you pay.

Which one fits you?

Milly B.’s line is the case for leaving Zapier once the bill climbs. Jordan W.’s line is the case for staying when a Zap already runs every day. Mael T.’s September 2026 review is a freelancer on Make’s free plan. Matyas Z.’s August 2026 review is the warning that Make takes work to set up. Daniel R. and connor Q. are the Microsoft case, including the point where a flow leaves Microsoft 365. Josh L. and Bhaswarjyoti B. are the Pabbly case: cheaper, and harder to learn.

A UK sole trader, a handful of jobs a month. A form on the website, a row in a sheet, and a short email. That is three steps. Zapier’s free plan stops at two, so the next Zapier step is Professional from $19.99 a month on yearly billing, with 750 tasks. Make’s free plan, 1,000 credits and no time limit, covers a few hundred of those runs if each run is a handful of credits. Watch the first month, because a schedule that reads the sheet also uses credits. The quote follow-up guide is a worked system in that shape. Stay on Zapier’s free plan only if you can do the job in one trigger and one action, and you stay under 100 tasks.

A small agency that already lives in Zapier. Rebuilding every Zap is the expensive part, and it is unpaid time. If the Zaps run and the Professional or Team bill is still tolerable, stay. Jordan W.’s review is that ordinary case. Move a new workflow to Make when it would have been a multi-step Zap and you want to see the credit cost on the free plan before you commit. The Make and Zapier comparison is the page for that bill. Pabbly is the one to trial beside it if the agency’s complaint is the task price and someone on the team will learn a different builder. Josh L.’s review is that trade, and Bhaswarjyoti B.’s review is the usability cost.

An office that already pays for Microsoft 365. Leave requests, approvals and a row in Excel are Daniel R.’s review. If the licence is Business Basic, Standard or Premium, Microsoft’s FAQ lists Power Automate as included for standard connectors. Paying for Make, Zapier or Pabbly on top is a second bill for work the Microsoft licence already covers. connor Q.’s review is the moment to leave: the flow needs an app outside Microsoft 365, and the connector is premium. Premium is £11.50 per user a month, paid yearly, before VAT. From that point, Make’s free plan is the cheaper experiment. The connector marks are in the Make and Power Automate comparison, checked the day before this page.

A privacy-minded owner who is comfortable with a server. n8n’s Community edition is free to self-host, on n8n’s docs, and the data stays on a machine you control. You also own the updates, the backups and the downtime. n8n Cloud Starter at €20 a month, billed annually, is the hosted version, and it costs more than Make’s Core at $9 before you count the exchange rate. Activepieces’ pricing page says you can use its cloud, including an EU region in Frankfurt, or your own servers, and that a run is one credit however many steps it has. The same page says its SOC 2 programme is in progress and that it will not claim a certificate it does not hold. Activepieces has no public rating in this article. Pick n8n if you already know you will self-host. Pick Activepieces if the one-credit-per-run rule and the Frankfurt option are the point, and you have checked the free-plan reset in an account. Where staying on Zapier is the better choice

Stay when a two-step Zap and 100 tasks a month cover the work. Stay when the account is already full of Zaps that run, and the cost of rebuilding them is larger than the subscription. Where Make is the better buy

Make is the better buy for most UK small businesses that have outgrown a two-step Zap and do not already have the job inside Microsoft 365. The free plan is 1,000 credits a month and, on Make’s comparison page, it is not time-limited. Core starts at $9 a month for 10,000 credits on that page, against Zapier Professional from $19.99 a month for 750 tasks on Zapier’s own article. Capterra’s 4.8 from 409 reviews is a shade above Zapier’s 4.7, on a much smaller sample, and 91% of those Make reviews are from small businesses. n8n is the better buy when you will self-host, or when each run has so many steps that Make’s per-step credits cost more than €20 a month. Pabbly is the better buy when you want 10,000 action steps at the $16 yearly rate in Pabbly’s pricing file, and you have time to learn the builder. Activepieces is the better buy when a whole run should cost one credit and you want the Frankfurt region or your own server. Power Automate is the better buy when Microsoft 365 already includes the flow. IFTTT is the better buy when two Applets are the entire requirement.
