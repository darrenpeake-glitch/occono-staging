How to Handle a Missed Call When You Work Alone

Affiliate note: Occono may earn a commission if you use the Make, Systeme.io, or beehiiv links below, at no extra cost to you.

You were on a job, driving, or with someone else. The phone rang. The part that gets lost is not the ring. It is the detail, which stays inside a recording until you are parked tonight.

This is a small system: a greeting that asks for four facts, a note you can read at the next break, and a callback you make yourself. A transcript lets you see the message without playing it through. It does not set the price, and it does not ring the person back.

What you are building

One greeting. One note per missed call. One callback, from you.

Use the transcript if your phone already makes one. If it does not, listen once and type the note. A handful of messages a day does not need another subscription, and it does not need an AI receptionist.

An answering voice that picks up for you, or rings people back, is a later decision. The greeting and the callback come first. If those two are unreliable, a new tool answers the wrong problem.

The greeting

Replace the default greeting. Say this, in your own name, at a normal pace:

Hello, you have reached [your name] at [your business]. I cannot take the call right now. Please leave your name, your number, the job you need doing, and the best time to call you back. I will return the call myself.

Four facts: name, number, job, and when to call. Leave out a list of services, a discount, and a request to follow you anywhere. The person already tried to reach you. Give them a clear place to talk.

On an iPhone, Apple’s set-up guide says the first time you open Voicemail you are asked for a voicemail password and a greeting. To change it later, use Edit Voicemail Greeting in the unified Phone layout, or Greeting in the classic layout, then choose Custom and record. Voicemail itself is available from select carriers in select countries or regions, so if the Phone app will not take a custom greeting, record the same words through the greeting your network already uses.

On any other phone, use the greeting controls in the Phone app or the network voicemail you already have. The words are the system. The handset is not.

The note

Write one note per message. A notes app, a scrap of paper, or a row in a sheet are all fine. Do it at the next break, not at the end of the week.

LineWhat you write
NameWhat they said. If the recording is unclear, write “name unclear” and still call the number.
NumberThe number you heard. Read it from the audio before you dial. A transcript is a prompt, not the phone number.
JobA few words they will recognise, such as “leaking kitchen tap” or “quote for the side return”.
WhenThe time they asked for. If they did not give one, write “same day” and call when you are free.

Then you dial, say who you are, and name the job they described. The note is so you can do that without replaying the message in a car park.

When an iPhone already transcribes it

Apple’s UK feature-availability page lists Live Voicemail for English (United Kingdom). Apple’s set-up guide also says Live Voicemail is not available in every country, region, language, or carrier. Check that page, then check the Phone app, before you assume it is on your handset.

To turn it on, Apple’s guide says: Settings, then Apps, then Phone, then Live Voicemail. When someone is leaving a message, the iPhone can show a transcription as they speak. You can pick up during that message. They cannot hear you unless you answer. Apple says calling and data rates may apply.

If the iPhone is turned off, or it is out of the carrier network’s range, Apple says the call goes to carrier voicemail if that is available. Live Voicemail is not a second copy of every message you will ever miss.

Apple’s check-voicemail guide says a transcription appears in the bottom half of the screen when you open a message. In the unified layout that is Calls, then the message, or the Voicemails list. In the classic layout it is Voicemail, then the message. Messages stay until you delete them, or until the carrier erases them if they are not stored in iCloud. In some countries, deleted messages are permanently erased, and changing the SIM can delete them too.

The same guide says that, with Apple Intelligence, you can see suggested actions from the transcript, such as a reminder or a calendar event. Those suggestions are turned on or off under Settings, Apps, Phone, Suggestions in Voicemail. Apple says the suggestions are available in English, and that Apple Intelligence is not available on every model, language, or region. Usage limits may apply. Open any suggested event and check the time against the recording before you treat it as booked.

Apple’s feature-availability page also lists Apple Intelligence voicemail summaries for English (United Kingdom), and the check-voicemail page says the Phone app can give you a summary. Use a summary as a heading. The number and the job still come from the recording.

One setting fights this system. Apple says that if Silence Unknown Callers is on, unknown numbers go straight to Live Voicemail and your phone does not ring first. New customers usually ring from numbers you have not saved. Leave that setting off unless you have decided you would rather take those messages than those calls. Apple also says calls its carrier has identified as spam do not go through Live Voicemail. They are declined.

Do not hide from new numbers

Apple’s unknown-callers guide puts three choices under Settings, Apps, Phone, Screen Unknown Callers:

  • Never. Numbers that are not in your contacts ring like any other call.
  • Ask Reason for Calling. Those calls are screened. The caller is asked why they are calling before your phone rings.
  • Silence. Those calls are silenced and sent to voicemail.

Never is the setting that lets a new enquiry ring. Silence sends that enquiry to voicemail. Ask Reason for Calling can be useful on a job, but only after you have heard it from the caller’s side. Ring your mobile from a number that is not in your contacts and see what the other person has to do.

Apple says standard calling and data rates may apply to call screening, calls are not screened while you are roaming, and calling the emergency services turns call screening off for 24 hours. On software earlier than iOS 17, Live Voicemail also has to be on to use call screening. Save people you already work with into Contacts, so they are not treated as unknown.

If the callback is too long to remember

A short job fits in the four-line note. A long one, with sizes, access, and a date, is easier if the callback itself is recorded and transcribed. You still make the call.

Apple’s record-and-transcribe guide describes recording a phone call with one other person, in some countries and regions. Before you start, Apple tells you to check the feature-availability page and to make sure the other person is willing to be recorded. During the call you tap Call Recording. Both people hear an audio notice that the call is being recorded. You stop by tapping again or by hanging up. The recording is saved to a Call Recordings folder in Notes.

Apple says call recording is not currently available in the European Union, or in a list of other countries that does not include the United Kingdom. The UK feature-availability page lists English under Phone: Call Recording, and English (United Kingdom) under Phone: Call Transcription. Check that page for your language and region anyway. A supported iPhone is also required for the Apple Intelligence summary of a recorded call, which the guide says you can read in Notes.

The transcript may not appear straight away. Apple’s own note is to verify it before you rely on it. It might not be a perfect representation of the conversation. Spoken words in supported languages are transcribed, and the speaker of each statement is identified. You can copy the transcript into the note. If a price or a date in the transcript is not one you heard, it is not part of the job.

The Call Recording option is on by default. A call is not recorded until you tap Call Recording on that call. Apple says you can turn the option off under Settings, Apps, Phone, Audio Call Recording.

The caller’s details

This is a reading of published guidance, not legal advice. The ICO says the Data (Use and Access) Act means some of the pages below are under review, so read the current versions before you rely on them.

A voicemail is a message the person chose to leave so you would call them back. A live recording is a different thing. Apple already plays an audio notice on its call-recording feature, and it tells you to record only if the other person is willing. Say why in your own words as well: you want the job details right. If they object, stop. The guide above tells you how to stop the recording.

The ICO’s lawful-basis guide says you need a lawful basis before you use personal information, you decide it before you start, and you include the basis and the purpose in your privacy information. Their voice, name, and number are personal information. This article does not choose a basis for you.

The ICO’s page on monitoring workers is written for employers. It says that recording calls also collects information about the people on the line, that you must tell those people you are recording and why, and that a recorded message is good practice. A sole trader with no staff is not running a staff-monitoring scheme. The person on the call is still a person. Read that page before you record anyone.

Keep the recording only while you need it for that job. Apple’s guide says deleting the audio recording in Notes also deletes the transcript. Do not paste a client’s recording into a random website to get a text version. If your phone will not transcribe, type the four lines from the audio.

Do not let a recording ring them back

Calling someone back about the job they just described is you answering them.

The ICO’s telephone-marketing guide describes an automated marketing call as a call made by an automated dialling system that plays a recorded message. You must not make one unless the person has specifically consented to automated calls from you. Consent for ordinary live calls is not enough. Every automated call has to include your name and a contact address or freephone number, and you have to display your number. That page is one of the pages the ICO says is under review.

The system in this article does not include a voice that dials people. If you later try a service that answers in a synthetic voice, or calls people back, ring it from your own phone first. Read that ICO page before it calls anyone else.

A form, when they hang up on voicemail

Some people will not leave a message. A short form is a second door. It does not replace the greeting, because the person who already has your number will still ring.

Systeme.io’s website guide describes a form element that can include a phone-number field and a custom field. Custom fields are created from the contacts page, then they show up in the form. The same guide says an automation on the form can send an email when someone submits it.

Their guide to emailing yourself about a new lead is written for an opt-in page. The trigger is “Funnel step form subscribed”. The action is “Send email to a specific email address”. You put your own address in the recipient field, and you can use the personalization substitutes in that guide so the email contains what the person typed. The button’s action has to be “Submit form”, or that email does not send. They tell you to test the page.

Ask for the same four facts: name, number, the job, and when to call. Submit it yourself, to an address you actually read, before you put the link anywhere. The website guide also has a double opt-in tick box. If you turn that on, read what the person has to do before you treat the request as received. Check the plan limits inside your Systeme.io account before you depend on the form. This article does not quote a price.
