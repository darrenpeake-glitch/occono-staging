Which Mailchimp alternative lets a UK small business send more before it pays?

Affiliate note: Occono may earn a commission if you use the Systeme.io, beehiiv or Make links on this page, at no extra cost to you. The Mailchimp, Kit, MailerLite and Brevo links are not affiliate links. Occono does not earn from those four.

Quick verdict

Best for a letter you already send, once the list is past a few hundred people: beehiiv if the letter is the product, or Kit if you want the widest free list. beehiiv’s free plan holds up to 2,500 subscribers and unlimited sends. Kit’s free plan holds up to 10,000 subscribers and unlimited broadcasts. We earn from beehiiv. We do not earn from Kit.

Best for a page, a checkout and one follow-up in the same login: Systeme.io. The free plan includes 2,000 contacts, three sales funnels, one course and one automation rule, and the pricing FAQ states 0% transaction fees. Prices on that page are in US dollars.

Best if you are happy to pay a small monthly bill in pounds: Brevo. Its pricing API lists Marketing Starter at £6 a month. We do not earn from Brevo.

Stay on Mailchimp if the list is still inside the free plan: up to 250 contacts, and up to 500 sends a month. Also stay if the journeys and the shop connection are already built, and the move would cost more than the subscription.

Which one fits you?

Julie Kinscheck’s October 2026 review is the Kit complaint in plain language: the paid plan feels steep for a one-person business. Amanda B’s review, from Great Britain the day before, is the Systeme.io side: support that answers a domain problem in clear language. Trustpilot’s Mailchimp summary is the other warning: billing and support, on a profile that invites reviews and still sits at 2.5.

A sole trader with about 300 customers. Mailchimp’s free plan does not hold them. The cap is 250 contacts. Essentials starts at $13 a month, and the help article says that starting price includes up to 500 contacts. MailerLite’s free plan is also 250 subscribers, so 300 people lands on Comfort, at $12 a month in USD or €11 in EUR on the first published tier. Kit’s free plan holds the 300 for broadcasts, and the sequence waits until Creator. Systeme.io’s free plan holds them, with one automation rule, inside 2,000 contacts. beehiiv’s free plan holds them as a newsletter, up to 2,500, and the automated sequence is on Lite. If the job is one letter a month, Kit or beehiiv is the free-plan fit. If the job is one chase email after an enquiry, Systeme.io’s free plan is the one that includes the rule. MailerLite includes automations on Free, and then stops you at 250 people.

A local shop with a Shopify or Etsy list. You want the customers you already have, not a second checkout. If the list is under 250 and you already send from Mailchimp, stay. The move is a chore, and Essentials at $13 is close to MailerLite Comfort at $12, so the saving is small. If the list is already past 250 and the letter is all you send, Kit’s free plan runs to 10,000 subscribers and beehiiv’s to 2,500. Neither is a till. If a new order should also land on a sheet, Make can connect the shop to the email tool. That is a second product. The Make and Zapier comparison on this site is the page for that bill.

A coach selling a short course. Systeme.io’s free plan includes one course and a checkout, and the pricing FAQ states no platform transaction fee. Kit can sell a digital file on the free plan, at the 3.5% + 30¢ on the pricing table. beehiiv’s pricing FAQ says digital products are on every plan, and the Lite plan description lists them again as something added on Lite, so do not treat a free-plan product as settled. If the thing you sell is the course, Systeme.io is the smaller stack. If the thing you sell is the weekly letter, Kit’s free plan holds far more subscribers before a monthly fee. The Kit and Systeme.io comparison goes further on that split, and the Systeme.io piece for solopreneurs stays with the all-in-one.

A newsletter writer. beehiiv is built for the letter: unlimited sends on the free plan, up to 2,500 subscribers, and paid subscriptions only once you move to Lite. Kit’s free plan is wider, at 10,000, and it is a broadcaster until you pay for sequences. Mailchimp’s free plan is the wrong shape for a newsletter that might grow, because 250 contacts and 500 sends a month run out on a single mail to a modest list. Substack is the other newsletter path, and this site’s beehiiv and Substack comparison is the page for the 10% cut.

Where Mailchimp is the better buy

Stay if the list is under 250 contacts and 500 sends a month is enough. You get one audience, basic templates, and a form. You do not get scheduling, and you do not get automation flows. For a quiet list and one mail a month, that can be the whole job, at $0, in a tool a customer may already recognise. The 14-day trial of Essentials or Standard is there if you want to see the paid screen, and the footnote says Mailchimp takes payment details before it starts.

Stay if the journeys, the segments and a shop connection are already running, and rebuilding them is the real cost. Essentials starts at $13 a month for a starting tier the help article describes as up to 500 contacts. Standard starts at $20. Those are US dollars. Mailchimp says a price in another currency is an estimate, and the amount charged depends on the exchange rate, with tax on top. Switching to save a few dollars, and then spending a week rebuilding one automation, is a bad trade. The Trustpilot score of 2.5 from 1,547 reviews is the reason to export the list anyway, and to read a renewal before you let a trial roll on.

Leave the free plan when the list passes 250, or when one mail to the whole list would break the 500-send cap. Kit and beehiiv hold a normal local list without a monthly fee. Systeme.io holds it and adds a page and a checkout. Brevo is the one that publishes a starter price in pounds, at £6 a month, once you have decided to pay. MailerLite is the middle: automations before you pay, then a wall at 250 subscribers.

Pick one list. Two tools means two places a customer can be subscribed, and an unsubscribe in one of them does not clear the other.
