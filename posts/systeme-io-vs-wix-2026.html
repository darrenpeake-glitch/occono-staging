The short version

Wix is a website builder with hosting, templates, a store and bookings on the paid cards that list them. Systeme.io is an all-in-one for funnels, email, a course and checkout. The first bill is the difference. Systeme.io’s free plan is $0, and its top published plan is $97 a month. Wix’s free site stays on a wixsite.com address until you buy Premium. The first Wix card that accepts payments is Core.

The second difference is email. Systeme.io’s visible FAQ says unlimited email sends on every plan, including free, with the free plan capped at 1 email campaign. Wix’s own help says you can send 200 marketing emails a month on the free email allowance, and that a larger allowance is a separate email-marketing plan. The third difference is the cut on a card payment. Systeme.io’s pricing page says 0% transaction fees and does not print Stripe’s or PayPal’s rates. Wix Payments’ UK card rate, on Wix’s service-fees page, is 2.1% + £0.20 for a credit or debit card other than American Express.

Wix’s email Core plan and Wix’s website Core plan are different products.

Sales cap on the free plan The help article says free accounts have a lifetime sales limit of $10,000 USD. A warning appears at $8,000. No sales cap of that kind is stated on the plans page we read. Selling starts when the plan allows payments, which is Core and above.

Wix: free site, no card, on a wixsite.com address. Premium is a paid upgrade with a 14-day refund on the first upgrade, in Wix’s FAQ. The cards we read show Light at $17.77, Core at $29.77, Business at $39.77 and Business Elite at $159.77, each with “mo” and “Price includes GST”. The disclaimer says those displayed prices are yearly USD reference prices and do not include tax. Wix’s plans page.

What a year of Systeme.io looks like, using only the monthly FAQ prices, and labelled as our arithmetic. Startup is $17 × 12 = $204. Webinar is $47 × 12 = $564. Unlimited is $97 × 12 = $1,164. The “2 months free” badge is not turned into a dollar total here, because the FAQ does not print one. Your card issuer may still add a conversion charge. The pricing page shows no VAT rate. Systeme.io’s subscription help says a VAT number can be stored on the billing details. It does not say UK VAT is added to the $17.

This article does not publish a yearly Wix total. If “mo” means a monthly charge, the Core card is $29.77 a month. If the disclaimer is the one that governs, that figure is a yearly subscription shown as a monthly equivalent, and tax is extra. “Price includes GST” is a third reading, and GST is not UK VAT. The purchase page is the figure to pay. Capterra’s $17 starting price matches the dollars on the Light card before the “.77”, and it does not show the cents.

The UK card fee is separate from the site plan, and it is in pounds. On a £47 workshop, 2.1% is £0.987, plus £0.20 is £1.187. Wix’s invoice article says processing fees are rounded to the nearest cent, so that is £1.19 on this arithmetic. Systeme.io’s own fee on that £47 is $0 on the pricing page’s 0% rule. Stripe or PayPal can still charge you. This article does not have Systeme.io’s processor rates. Two sales of £47 on Wix Payments are two lots of £0.20, plus 2.1% of £94. Which one fits you?

A Leeds career coach with a one-page offer, about 150 people on a list, and no product for sale yet. Systeme.io’s free plan covers the page, the list and unlimited sends at $0, with 1 email campaign until you upgrade. Wix’s free site does not include your own domain. Light is the card that does, and it cannot take a payment. Wix’s free email allowance is 200 messages a month, so one send to 150 people uses most of it. If the list is the job, Systeme.io. If clients are choosing you from the look of the site, Wix Light is the published starting point, and you still need somewhere for a list larger than that 200. A Manchester consultant selling a £97 workbook to a list of about 80. Systeme.io’s free plan includes the checkout and a 0% platform fee until the $10,000 lifetime sales cap, which this article does not convert into pounds. Wix needs Core before the site can take the payment. Core’s card shows $29.77 with “mo”, and each card payment then picks up 2.1% + £0.20 if you use Wix Payments in the UK. Systeme.io, unless the designed site is why people buy the workbook. Anne S.’s review, above, is the objection to Systeme.io’s page builder if you already like building on Wix.

A Bristol yoga teacher who wants a class schedule on the site and payment for a workshop. Wix Core is the first card that lists payments, basic eCommerce, and scheduling. The email allowance is still 200 marketing emails a month unless you buy the separate email plan, and that plan’s price was not on the pages we read. Systeme.io’s pricing page has a booking-calendar row and does not show the cell values, so this article does not claim an event limit. If the diary and the site are the product, Wix Core is the published starting point, after you read the purchase page. If the workshop is really a small course plus a handful of emails, Systeme.io’s free plan is the one with a course, a funnel and no site-plan bill. Where Wix is the better buy

When people hire you because of the site, and you want the editor, the templates, the hosting and a domain voucher on a yearly Premium plan. Reviewers who like it talk about building without code, and about bookings and a store in the same place. Reviewers who complain talk about the mobile layout, about being stuck on a template, and about features that sit on a higher plan. Mandeep A.’s line is the booking site. Bruno L.’s line is the template lock. Amy C.’s line is the premium-account wall. Light is the card if you are not taking money on the site. Core is the card if you are. Confirm the amount and the currency on the purchase page, because the plans page disagrees with itself, and because this check did not load a sterling subscription price.

Where Systeme.io is the cheaper buy

For a UK sole trader who is still proving an offer, and for most people under 2,000 contacts who need the list and the checkout more than a brochure site. You can put the page, the emails and the payment on the free plan, and move to Startup at $17 a month when the list, the student cap, the single campaign or the $10,000 sales cap says you have to. You also get the funnel in the same login. Anne S. switched from Wix and found Systeme.io’s page builder more limited. Jessica S.’s line is the other gap: buyer emails that cannot be branded.

Before you pay Wix for a year, open the purchase page and read the currency, the tax line and whether the figure is the amount due today or a monthly equivalent. The plans page we read supports more than one of those readings. On Systeme.io, export a note of your lifetime sales before you rely on the free plan past a few thousand dollars, because the help article stops new sales at $10,000.
