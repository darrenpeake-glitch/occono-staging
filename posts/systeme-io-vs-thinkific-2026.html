Quick summary

Start on the free plan, and pay for a school only once the course is selling

Best for a first course, before you know it will sell: Systeme.io. The free plan needs no card. It includes one course, up to 500 students, up to 2,000 contacts and, in the page’s FAQ data, up to $10,000 in total sales. The pricing FAQ states 0% transaction fees. We earn from Systeme.io.

Best when buyers need a school, and sales already cover the plan: Thinkific Start. There is no free plan. There is a 30-day trial. On 21 October 2026 the pricing page showed Start at $109 a month, or $82 a month billed annually. Start lists unlimited courses, quizzes, assignments, certificates and one community. Thinkific branding stays until Grow. We do not earn from Thinkific.

Thinkific is the more expensive place to find out whether anyone will buy. Systeme.io is the cheaper place to teach, until the course itself is what people are paying for.

The offer

Systeme.io. Free plan: $0, no card. One course, up to 500 students, up to 2,000 contacts, unlimited email sends, and a $10,000 total-sales cap in the FAQ data. Startup is $17 a month. Webinar is $47 a month. Unlimited is $97 a month. Annual billing is on the page and badged “2 months free”. This article does not turn that badge into a dollar price, because the plan data it could read gives the monthly figures. There is no separate paid-plan trial. If you cancel a paid plan, the FAQ data says you keep it until the next billing date, then the account moves back to the free plan, and the payment is non-refundable. If you are then over the free limits, the account is restricted until you upgrade or remove the extra items. Nothing is deleted, including student progress, on that same answer.

Thinkific. No free plan on the pricing FAQ. A 30-day trial of Start, Grow and Expand. Start is $109 a month, or $82 a month on annual billing. Grow is $219 a month, or $164 a month on annual billing. Expand is $499 a month, or $374 a month on annual billing. Plus is custom. The pricing page says annual billing saves 25%. You can cancel during the trial, on the FAQ. The price-update article says you can cancel at any time without charge through account settings, and that a pro-rata refund of an annual plan after renewal depends on whether your jurisdiction requires it. This article does not turn that into UK legal advice. The same article’s new pound prices, for people billed in pounds, are Start at £92 a month or £828 a year, and Grow at £186 a month or £1,668 a year. The pricing page itself showed dollars when it was read. Thinkific is a Canadian company. Its cost article says a card issuer may add a cross-border fee, usually under $1, which Thinkific does not charge, and that VAT may be added on top of the subscription. It does not state the VAT rate.

A worked example, Occono’s own arithmetic. It uses the dollar prices above. It ignores VAT, the cross-border card fee, and payment processing. Twelve times Thinkific Start at $109 is $1,308. Thinkific’s price-update article lists the annual Start total as $978. $1,308 minus $978 is $330, which matches the “Save $330” label on the pricing card. Twelve times the card’s $82 is $984, which is $6 more than $978. $978 divided by 12 is $81.50, and the card rounds that to $82. Twelve times Systeme.io Startup at $17 is $204. That is the monthly price held for a year. It is not an annual-billing total, because Systeme.io’s page did not show one that this article could read.

One course at $97, sold to 10 people, is $970. That does not cover a year of Thinkific Start at the $978 figure. It does cover a year of Systeme.io Startup at $204, and it is inside the free plan’s $10,000 sales cap. The free plan’s 500-student cap is a different exit. At $20 a course, 500 students is $10,000, so the two free-plan caps arrive together. At $97, the sales cap arrives first: 103 sales is $9,991, and 104 sales is $10,088. Stripe or Thinkific Payments still take their own fee out of those sales. The $970 is not money you keep in full.

Which one fits you?

Harshit V.’s September 2026 review is a person who wanted email, pages and automation in one place, and who found the screens slow to learn. The anonymous January 2026 review is aimed at a one-person business that does not want a stack of tools. Daphnie C.’s August 2026 review is an organisation delivering training, happy with the course tools and still exporting data for follow-up. Monica Kearse and WeHo Guy are creators who paid and then could not get the student experience, or their files, to behave. Those four are closer to a coach deciding where to put a course than a software-buyer average is.

A self-employed coach selling a first workshop to people who already enquire. You do not yet know if ten people will pay. Systeme.io’s free plan is the one that lets you put up a page, a checkout and one course before a monthly bill. The caps are the point to read twice: 500 students, 2,000 contacts, and $10,000 in total sales. A local workshop will not hit 500 students quickly. A $97 course can hit the sales cap in about a hundred sales, on the arithmetic above. Startup at $17 a month is the next step, and it is still far under Thinkific Start. Thinkific’s 30 days are enough to build the course. They are a short window if the first sale takes a term. The pricing page does not say the trial is free of a card.

A trainer whose buyers expect a quiz, a certificate, or a live cohort. Thinkific’s Start card lists those. Systeme.io’s pricing page does not. Daphnie C.’s review is the case for paying: course and learner admin in one place, with reporting that still needed a spreadsheet. If the certificate is part of what the buyer pays for, Start at $82 a month on annual billing, or $109 month to month, is the bill for that school. Branding stays on Start. Phone support and bulk email to students wait until Grow, at $164 a month on annual billing or $219 month to month. On a UK Thinkific Payments account, budget 1.7% + £0.20 on a card sale, and another 0.7% if the buyer is on a payment plan. If you keep Stripe instead, Thinkific adds 2% on Start for a UK creator. That 2% is not a Stripe fee. It is Thinkific’s gateway fee. The fee article says this fee applies only to the first $1 million in sales made through Thinkific in a calendar year, and is waived after that.

A consultant who needs the sales page and one follow-up more than a campus. Systeme.io is that stack: funnels, email and a course, with three funnels and one automation rule before you pay. Thinkific Start does include landing pages, a site builder and email automation. It does not include bulk email to students. If the letter is the product and the course is occasional, a newsletter tool is the closer question. The beehiiv and Substack comparison is that page, and beehiiv is the newsletter tool this site uses. If a new enrolment should also land on a spreadsheet you already keep, Make can connect the form to the sheet. That is a second product, and a second bill once you leave Make’s free plan.

Someone moving a course that already sells. Export the list, the files and the student record before you cancel anything. WeHo Guy’s September 2026 review is a creator who stopped paying and could not get the course materials back through support. Thinkific’s reply says admin access ends when the subscription ends, and that video files can still be downloaded from the Video Library. Monica Kearse’s October 2026 review is a different failure: students who could not open the lessons. Read those against Daphnie C.’s review, which is a training operation that found the platform dependable. If the student area is already the reason people buy, and the monthly bill is covered, Thinkific is the tool built for that school. If you are moving because the bill is the problem, Systeme.io Startup at $17 a month is the smaller number. The Kajabi comparison is the next page if you already have revenue and you want a more polished student experience than either of these entry plans.

Where Thinkific is the better buy

Thinkific wins when the course is the product. Quizzes, assignments, certificates, cohorts and a custom domain are on Start, not held back for a higher plan. The comparison table allows 10,000 current students on that same plan. Capterra’s 4.4 from 207 reviews, and Daphnie C.’s August 2026 review, are the public case that the teaching tools work for people running training. You pay for that from the first month after the trial. There is no free plan on the pricing FAQ, whatever the page’s short description says. Start at $82 a month on annual billing is $978 a year in Thinkific’s own price-update table. Grow, at $164 a month on annual billing, is the first plan that removes Thinkific branding and includes bulk email. The Trustpilot score of 1.8 from 868 reviews is the reason to test a real checkout, and to download the video library, before you point adverts at the school. Recent reviews on that profile are about access and cancellation, not about the course builder. The all-time star split is much kinder than 1.8, and only 30 reviews are from the last 12 months.

Systeme.io wins when the offer is still unproven, or when you would otherwise pay for a page builder, an email tool and a course host. The free plan is a real account: one course, 500 students, unlimited sends, no card. The $10,000 sales cap and the 6-month inactivity line are in the page’s FAQ data, next to a visible FAQ that says the free plan never expires. Read both on the day you start. Startup at $17 a month is the first paid step, against Thinkific’s $109. The platform fee is stated as 0%. You still pay Stripe or PayPal, and this page does not pretend to know the UK rate those two will charge you. Capterra’s 4.8 from 2,131 reviews is the public score this article could read. Trustpilot for Systeme.io is not in the table, because the page did not load. Harshit V.’s review is the learning-curve warning. Export the list if you later move.

Use one checkout. Two course platforms means two places a buyer can pay, and two student lists to export when you leave.
