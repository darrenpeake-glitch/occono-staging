Quick summary

Use the Power Automate you already pay for inside Microsoft 365. Use Make when the job leaves Microsoft.

Best when the work lives in Outlook, Excel, SharePoint and Teams: Power Automate. Microsoft’s licensing FAQ lists Office 365 Business Basic, Business Standard and Business Premium among the licences that include Power Automate. That included use covers cloud flows with standard connectors, up to 6,000 actions a day per user. Premium and custom connectors are not included. We do not earn from Microsoft.

Best when the job touches Xero, Stripe, Mailchimp or Calendly, or when nobody in the business has a Microsoft 365 work account: Make. Make’s Free plan is 1,000 credits a month, and Make says it is not time-limited. Make’s own comparison page lists Core at $9 a month, starting at 10,000 credits. On Power Automate, the Stripe, Mailchimp and Calendly connectors are marked Premium, and so are the Xero connectors on Microsoft’s list. Power Automate Premium is £11.50 per user a month, paid yearly, before VAT. We earn from Make.

What a unit of work is Credits. Make’s FAQ says each module action in a scenario uses credits, and most actions use 1. Reading, searching, creating, updating and deleting data in an app all count. Router and error-handler modules do not. Some AI features may use more. Actions per day. The FAQ gives 6,000 a day for Power Automate Free, Microsoft 365 and all trials, and 40,000 a day for Premium. Limits apply per user and do not pool across a business. Connectors also have their own rate limits: the FAQ’s example is SharePoint at 600 actions a minute.
When you run out Scenarios stop until credits are added. Incoming webhooks queue up to your queue size. You can upgrade, buy extra credits in bundles of 1,000 or 10,000, or turn on automatic purchases of 10,000 on Core, Pro and Teams. Credits expire at the end of the term. The FAQ says a flow that runs too many actions is throttled, with a banner on the flow, and suggests a Process licence for flows that run thousands of actions a day. A Process licence is 250,000 actions a day.

The offer

Power Automate. If your business already has Microsoft 365 Business Basic, Standard or Premium, Microsoft’s FAQ lists those among the licences that include Power Automate, with standard connectors and up to 6,000 actions a day per user. There is also a Power Automate Free licence for work or school accounts. The UK pricing page offers a 30-day trial. Microsoft’s deep dive describes a 90-day trial that a user starts in the portal. Premium is £11.50 per user a month, paid yearly, before VAT. Twelve months of Premium is £138 per user, before VAT. If your business is not VAT registered, adding UK VAT at 20% makes that £13.80 a month, or £165.60 a year. Those totals are Occono’s arithmetic from the page price. Microsoft says the actual price is the one shown at checkout.

Make. Free plan: $0, 1,000 credits a month, not time-limited. Make’s comparison page lists Core at $9 a month starting at 10,000 credits, Pro at $16 and Team at $29. That page does not say which billing period the prices are for, and the plan cards on Make’s pricing page did not load for this article, so Occono does not turn $9 into a yearly total. Capterra lists Core at $10.59 a month. Make’s FAQ lists major credit cards, ACH direct debit and Google Pay for paid plans. If you cancel, the paid plan runs to the end of the term and the account then moves to the Free plan.

A worked example, Occono’s own arithmetic. A website enquiry arrives, is added as a row in Excel, gets an email reply, and a message is posted in Teams. On Make that is a trigger and three actions, so about 4 credits per enquiry, using Make’s rule that most actions use 1 credit. One hundred enquiries a month is about 400 credits, which is under the Free plan’s 1,000. Make’s FAQ also counts reading data from an app as a credit, so a trigger that checks on a schedule can use credits even in a quiet week. Watch the first month’s usage. On Power Automate, Outlook, Excel and Teams are Microsoft’s own apps, and 400 actions a month is far below the 6,000-a-day limit. If the same flow added a Xero contact or took a Stripe payment, the connector would be Premium, and the cheapest route on Microsoft’s UK page is £11.50 per user a month.

Which one fits you?

Daniel R.’s July 2026 review is the case for Power Automate: approvals, reminders and notifications across Outlook, Teams, Excel and SharePoint. connor Q.’s April 2026 review is the warning about leaving Microsoft’s apps. Mael T.’s September 2026 review is a freelancer on Make’s free version. Matyas Z.’s August 2026 review and Wayne Thompson’s September 2026 Trustpilot review are both about how hard setup can be on Make.

An office manager in a five-person firm that runs on Microsoft 365. Leave requests come in by email, go into a SharePoint list, and the manager approves in Teams. That is Microsoft’s own ground. If your plan is one of the Business licences Microsoft lists, Power Automate is already part of it, the connectors are standard, and the limit is 6,000 actions a day per user. Paying for Make on top would be a second bill for work you already have. Check who owns the flow first. Microsoft’s licence types page says the Free licence runs cloud flows without sharing, and the FAQ says action limits are per user, not pooled across the business.

A UK sole trader with Xero, a Stripe link and a Gmail address. A paid invoice in Xero should send a thank-you email and add a row to a sheet. The Xero and Stripe connectors on Power Automate are Premium. A personal Gmail address is not the work or school account the Free licence is described for. Make has a Verified Xero app, and the Free plan’s 1,000 credits a month will cover a small number of invoices if each run is a few steps. The quote follow-up guide is a worked system in the same shape, using a sheet and two chase emails.

A coach or trainer who wants a booking page, a sales page and a course. Neither tool is the product. They connect products. Calendly is a Premium connector on Power Automate. If the real need is a page, email and a course in one place, an all-in-one such as Systeme.io may remove the need for the automation. The Systeme.io and Thinkific comparison covers that choice. Add Make only for the gap the all-in-one does not cover.

A shop or creator who wanted Mailchimp in a flow. Mailchimp is a Premium connector on Power Automate, so the newsletter step is the one that would push you to £11.50 a month. If you are choosing a newsletter tool anyway, beehiiv has a free plan. The beehiiv and Kit comparison and the Mailchimp alternatives guide have the limits, checked earlier this week.

A bookkeeper who retypes figures from an old Windows program. If the program has no connector and no API, Power Automate’s desktop flows can click through it like a person. Premium includes one attended bot, at £11.50 per user a month before VAT. Unattended runs, with nobody at the PC, need a Process licence at £115.30 per bot a month. Make’s pages read for this article do not describe that kind of desktop automation.

Where Power Automate is the better buy

Power Automate wins when the business already pays for Microsoft 365 Business and the job stays inside Microsoft’s apps or the standard connectors. Microsoft’s FAQ lists Business Basic, Standard and Premium among the licences that include it. The limit is 6,000 actions a day per user. Gmail, Google Sheets, Slack, Trello, Typeform, WordPress, DocuSign and Twilio are Standard connectors on Microsoft’s pages. Desktop flows are the one thing in this comparison that Make’s pages do not describe. Capterra’s 4.4 from 239 reviews, and Daniel R.’s review, are the public case. connor Q.’s review, and Bonnie B.’s June 2026 three-star review on the same page, which says “the UI needs significant work”, are the reasons to try one flow before you plan ten.

Where Make is the better buy

Make wins when the job crosses into apps Microsoft prices as Premium, or when there is no Microsoft 365 work account to start from. Stripe, Mailchimp and Calendly are Premium on Power Automate, and so are the two Xero connectors on Microsoft’s list. Make’s Free plan is 1,000 credits a month with no time limit, and Make’s own comparison page puts Core at $9 a month from 10,000 credits, against Power Automate Premium at £11.50 per user a month, paid yearly, before VAT. Capterra’s 4.8 from 409 reviews is the higher of the two Capterra scores. Make’s Trustpilot score of 2.6 from 170 reviews, with 61 one-star reviews, is the warning, and most of the complaints quoted above are about how hard setup is. The Make and Zapier comparison and the n8n and Make comparison cover the other two tools people weigh against Make.

Start with one flow you already do by hand every week. Count its steps before you choose. The step count is what both tools charge for.
