beehiiv or Substack: which newsletter platform lets a UK creator keep more of what they earn?

Affiliate note: Occono may earn a commission if you use the beehiiv or Make links on this page, at no extra cost to you. The Substack links are not affiliate links. Occono does not earn from Substack.

Quick verdict

Best for writers who want reach, and no monthly fee: Substack. Publishing is free at any number of subscribers. Readers can find you in the Substack app and through other publications' recommendations. If you turn on paid subscriptions, Substack keeps 10% of that revenue. We do not earn from Substack.

Best for a small business or creator who wants their own site, automations, and to keep subscription revenue once it covers a monthly plan: beehiiv. Paid subscriptions are not on the free plan. On Lite and above, beehiiv says it takes 0% of paid subscription revenue. You pay the plan, and you pay Stripe. At the prices checked on 19 October 2026, Lite is $49 a month on yearly billing for up to 1,000 subscribers.

beehiiv does not keep more at every size. On our own arithmetic, below, Substack's 10% is the smaller bill until subscription revenue is about $490 a month.

The offer

beehiiv. Free plan: $0, no card, up to 2,500 subscribers, unlimited sends. Lite and Pro include a free trial, with no card, and the trial ends when you upgrade. The page does not say how many days that trial lasts, so this article does not state a length. Some paid features are marked “not in trial”, including the 0% take rate, the ad network, and removing beehiiv branding. With the page set to 1,000 subscribers and yearly billing, on 19 October 2026, Lite showed $49 a month and Pro showed $95 a month. The pricing data on that URL lists the yearly totals at that tier as $588 for Lite and $1,140 for Pro, and the monthly prices as $59 and $109. The price steps up with the list. At up to 2,500 subscribers the same data lists Lite at $79 a month, or $69 a month on yearly billing ($828 for the year). Lite is sold through 100,000 subscribers. Pro is sold through 250,000. Enterprise is custom.

Substack. Publishing is free at any subscriber count on the cost article. There is no monthly plan price on that page. Paid subscriptions cost 10% of subscription revenue, plus Stripe. An optional custom domain is a one-time $50. Readers who subscribe in the iOS app may pay more than the web price. No separate trial of a paid Substack plan is described on the cost page, because the publishing account itself is free.

A worked example, Occono's own arithmetic. It uses the US dollar prices above. It ignores Stripe, tax, and the iOS markup. One hundred paying subscribers at $5 a month is $500 a month. Substack's 10% of that is $50 a month. beehiiv Lite, at the up-to-1,000 tier on yearly billing, is $49 a month, and beehiiv's stated cut of the $500 is $0. At this size the two platform costs are about the same, before Stripe. The crossover is $490 a month of subscription revenue: 10% of $490 is $49. Under that, Substack's 10% is the smaller bill. Forty people at $5 is $200 a month, and 10% of that is $20, which is less than $49. The $49 figure only holds while the whole list, free and paid, stays in the up-to-1,000 bucket. Past 1,000, the Lite yearly price in the pricing data is $69 a month at the next published tier.

Which one fits you?

Scott Weldon's September 2026 review, quoted above, is a local newsletter that wanted the site and the subscriber tools in one place. Khila James's October 2026 review is the other side: a free-plan limit changed, and the help pages did not all say the same thing. James Whiter's review is a reader who was billed and could not see an easy way back. Scott Weldon and Khila James are closer to a one-person publisher than a software-buyer score is.

A café or a trades business, sending one letter a month to people who already buy from you. You are not charging for the letter. Both free plans cover that. Substack does not cap the subscriber count on the cost page. beehiiv's free plan stops at 2,500, which a local list will not hit quickly. Neither takes a cut if nobody is paying for the newsletter. The Substack app will not fill the tables. If the letter should also live on your own web address, Substack's custom domain is a one-time $50. beehiiv's comparison table puts custom domains on Lite and above, even though the Free feature list also names them. If the letter later has to update a booking sheet, Make can connect beehiiv to the other tool. That is a second product, and a second bill once you leave Make's free plan.

A freelance writer starting a paid newsletter. Discovery is the part Substack is built for: the app, notes, and recommendations from other publications, with no monthly fee. You give up 10% once readers pay. Twenty people at $5 a month is $100, and 10% of that is $10. Forty people is $20. Both are under beehiiv Lite at $49 a month. beehiiv keeps more of each payment only after the 10% is larger than the plan, and only if the list is still in a tier you can afford. If being found matters more than the cut this year, start on Substack. Move if the 10% is clearly the dearer line, and export the list before you do. Khila James's review is the reason to read the current limits on the day you move, not the limits from a screenshot last month.

A coach selling a digital product next to the letter. beehiiv's FAQ says digital products are on every plan, and the comparison table lists one product on Free and three on Lite. Substack's help, on the pages checked, does not describe a product shop. If the product is a single file and the letter is the business, beehiiv can hold both, with the free-plan contradiction noted above. If you need a sales page, a checkout, and a course, a newsletter tool is the wrong stack. The Kit and Systeme.io comparison on this site is the closer question.

A hobby writer who wants readers, not a brand site. Substack. Publishing is free, the app is where other readers already are, and you pay nothing until you charge. The 10% only starts when you turn paid subscriptions on. beehiiv's free plan is a reasonable writing tool up to 2,500 subscribers, and it includes a simple site. It does not put you in the Substack app. Use beehiiv if you already know who the readers are. Use Substack if you do not.

Where Substack is the better buy

Substack wins when the list is small, when you want strangers to find the writing, and when you do not want a monthly bill. Free publishing at any subscriber count is a wider free plan than beehiiv's 2,500. The app, notes, and recommendations are the product. The 10% is the price of that, and it is cheaper than Lite until subscription revenue is about $490 a month. The Trustpilot score of 1.4 from 182 reviews is the reason to watch billing. Turn paid subscriptions on only when you mean to, and read how a reader cancels before you ask anyone else to subscribe. The iOS price can be higher than the price you set on the web.

beehiiv wins when the newsletter is part of a small business: a site, a referral programme, automations, and a product, and when the subscription money is large enough that 10% would cost more than the plan. Lite at $49 a month on yearly billing, for up to 1,000 subscribers, is the first plan that includes paid subscriptions and the 0% take rate. You still pay Stripe. The ad network is not promised in every country, and it is marked as outside the trial. Removing the beehiiv branding waits until Pro, at $95 a month on yearly billing for that same 1,000-subscriber tier. The free trial of Lite and Pro does not have a published length on beehiiv's pricing page. Custom domains are Lite and above on the comparison table, despite the Free list. Khila James's review, and that contradiction, are the same warning: read the comparison table on the day you pay, and export the list if the limits move.

Pick one. Two newsletters means two lists, and a reader who unsubscribes from one of them is still on the other.
