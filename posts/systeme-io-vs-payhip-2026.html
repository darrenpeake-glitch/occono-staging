The short version

Systeme.io is an all-in-one for funnels, email, a course and checkout, priced as a monthly plan in US dollars, with a free plan that caps contacts, funnels, courses and lifetime sales. Payhip is a store for digital products, courses, coaching, memberships and physical goods, priced as a percentage of the sale, with a monthly plan that reduces or removes that percentage. The first difference is the bill. Systeme.io’s free plan is $0 and its top published plan in the visible FAQ is $97 a month, and the FAQ says 0% transaction fees on every plan. Payhip’s free plan is also $0 a month, and then 5% of the sale. Plus is $29 plus 2%. Pro is $99 with no Payhip transaction fee. Both pricing pages are in dollars. Neither showed a pound subscription price.

The second difference is what the free plan includes. Systeme.io’s visible FAQ gives the free plan 1 course, 1 email campaign, 1 automation rule and 500 students, and the help article stops new payment-page sales at $10,000 of lifetime revenue. Payhip’s pricing page says all features, unlimited products and unlimited revenue on Free Forever as well as on Plus and Pro. The billing article says you do not upgrade to unlock a feature. You upgrade to change the percentage. Payhip’s courses page then adds a separate $5 a month if Payhip hosts the course videos. That $5 is not on the pricing cards.

The third difference is tax on a digital sale to a UK or EU buyer. Payhip’s pricing FAQ says it collects, reports and remits digital EU/UK VAT. Its VAT feature page says that, for a digital product bought in the EU or the UK, Payhip acts as the reseller and is responsible for that VAT, and that it reports and submits the amounts each quarter. Systeme.io’s UK VAT help, updated 1 November 2026, says a UK-based seller is charged 20% on digital sales to UK customers, for consumers and for UK businesses. It does not say Systeme.io remits that 20%, and it does not mention a registration threshold. Those are different jobs. This page is not tax advice, and it does not turn either vendor’s dollar prices into pounds.
