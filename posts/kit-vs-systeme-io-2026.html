The offer

Kit. Free plan: $0, up to 10,000 subscribers, unlimited forms, landing pages, and broadcasts. Sequences and visual automations start on Creator. Creator and Pro each have a 14-day trial with no card. Creator starts at $39 a month, or $33 a month when you pay $390 for the year, for up to 1,000 subscribers. Pro starts at $79 a month on the help-centre list, for the same 1,000-subscriber tier. Annual billing is described as two months free. After 1,000 subscribers the price steps up with the list. Selling through Kit costs 3.5% + 30¢ on the pricing table.

Systeme.io. Free plan: $0, no card, and it does not expire. The published limits are 2,000 contacts, three funnels, one course, one blog, one affiliate programme, one custom domain, unlimited emails, one automation rule, one email campaign, and 500 students. Startup is $17 a month. Webinar is $47 a month. Unlimited is $97 a month. Annual billing is two months free. The page states a 0% transaction fee.

Which one fits you?

Julie Kinscheck’s October 2026 review, quoted above, is the Kit complaint in plain language: the paid plan feels steep for a one-person business. Heather Boss and Mike Grainger, also quoted above, are the Systeme.io split: one login and a fair price, with parts of the screen that are hard if you are not technical.

A tradesperson who wants the quote form to chase itself. You need a page on a phone and one follow-up email, not a newsletter. Systeme.io’s free plan includes one automation rule and unlimited emails, inside 2,000 contacts and three funnels. Kit’s free plan will host the form and a broadcast. The automated sequence sits on Creator, from $39 a month. For a single chase email, Systeme.io is the free-plan fit. If you later want that form to update a spreadsheet as well, Make can join the two. That is a second tool, and a second bill once you leave Make’s free plan.

A coach selling a short course. Systeme.io’s free plan includes one course and a checkout, and the pricing page states no platform transaction fee. Kit can sell a digital file on the free plan, at 3.5% + 30¢, and the pricing page does not describe a course area or a student limit in the way Systeme.io does. If the thing you sell is the course, Systeme.io is the smaller stack. If the thing you sell is the weekly letter, and the course is a PDF, Kit’s free plan holds far more subscribers before you pay a monthly fee. Mike Grainger’s review is the practical warning: leave an afternoon for the first funnel.

A shop owner who already takes payment at the till. You want a list of past customers, not a second checkout. Kit’s free plan covers broadcasts up to 10,000 subscribers. Systeme.io’s free plan stops at 2,000 contacts, which is enough for a quiet shop and tight if the list is already larger. Kit’s price complaint starts when you need sequences, or when you pass 10,000. If you only want to publish the letter, beehiiv is another newsletter tool. It is not a funnel or a course platform, so it does not stand in for Systeme.io when the offer has several steps.

Where Kit is the better buy

Kit wins when the list is the business. Ten thousand subscribers before a monthly fee is a wider free plan than Systeme.io’s 2,000 contacts. Broadcasts, tags, and forms are on that free plan. The writing tools are the reason to pick it, and this page does not earn a commission if you do.

The bill changes when you need a sequence. Creator starts at $39 a month even if you only have a few hundred subscribers, because the published entry price is the “up to 1,000” tier. Julie Kinscheck’s review is that moment. The legacy ConvertKit Trustpilot profile, at 1.9 from 67 reviews, is also worth reading beside the newer kit.com profile at 4.0 from 161. And several September 2026 reviews on the kit.com profile describe accounts closed with little warning. Export the list if you are between tools.

Systeme.io wins when you would otherwise pay for a page builder, an email tool, and a course host. The free plan can carry one real offer: three funnels, one course, one automated rule, and no platform fee on the payment. Email depth is the weak side. One campaign and one automation rule will feel small as soon as the letter matters more than the checkout. The 4.9 Trustpilot score is what the page showed in October 2026, with Trustpilot’s own caution that the company has not been inviting reviews.

Most one-person businesses need one of these on day one. Add the second when the first one is clearly missing a step you do every week.
